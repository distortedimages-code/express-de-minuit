The basement archive of a Fleet Street newspaper, called the morgue: endless leaning shelves of bound newspaper volumes vanishing into darkness, a smell of ink and glue, a single hanging bulb. Behind a high desk, Mrs Pike, an old stern archivist with round glasses, a tight grey bun and black sleeve protectors. The only red: one bound volume with a red leather spine.

Style: still frame from a 1920s German Expressionist silent film. Painted theatrical set with distorted perspective: leaning walls, jagged slanted windows and doors, crooked furniture, sharp diagonal shapes. Hard theatrical light from one side, deep black shadows, dramatic shadows painted directly on the walls. Black and white orthochromatic film, high contrast, silver grain, faint scratches and dust, soft vignette, slightly uneven exposure. Only one element in saturated deep red; everything else in black, white and grey. Photographic realism, cinematic, eerie, quiet dread, 1923. Panoramic cinema frame, 2.4:1, composed for its full width: use the width for the set, the shadows and the depth. Keep faces (including the top of the head) and key subjects at least 12% away from the left and right edges, since phones show the picture slightly cropped at the sides. No text, no letters, no logos, no watermark, no border.
