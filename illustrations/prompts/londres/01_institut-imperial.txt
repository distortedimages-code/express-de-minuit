A grand banquet in the long gallery of the Imperial Institute in London, 1923: dinner jackets, evening gowns, braided military uniforms, gleaming sabres, waxed moustaches, seen as rows of dark heads and shoulders. At the far end, a large white projection screen and a magic-lantern projector. In the foreground on the left, Professor Julius Smith, 64, big walrus moustache and enormous bushy grey sideburns, puffs on a briar pipe; behind him stands his butler, gaunt and pale, straight as a rod. In the crowd, a dark-haired man of forty with a thick moustache stares straight at the viewer over a champagne glass. The only red: a heavy red velvet curtain beside the screen.

Style: still frame from a 1920s German Expressionist silent film. Hard theatrical light from one side, deep black shadows, dramatic shadows painted directly on the walls. Black and white orthochromatic film, high contrast, silver grain, faint scratches and dust, soft vignette, slightly uneven exposure. Only one element in saturated deep red; everything else in black, white and grey. Photographic realism, cinematic, eerie, quiet dread, 1923.
